A leakless House of Einherjar

fern
2025-12-27
ctf

Introduction

Hi everyone, today I will be showcasing a novel PoC that allows for the combination of both House of Water and Poison Null Byte to deliver leakless arbitrary write from just a single null byte overflow (einherjar-like primitive), along with a simple sample challenge to apply that PoC.

Here are the conditions needed to apply the attack:

  • Can malloc chunks of arbitrary size (up to 0x20000 roughly)
  • Can perform partial overrides
  • Can write at any offset within a chunk
  • Have one single null byte overflow (this is our opening primitive, we only use it once)

This attack has been tested in glibc 2.35, 2.39, 2.40. Does not work in 2.41 and 2.42, though I believe it can be trivially modified to.

I recommend you have a rough understanding of the main house of water attack first before proceeding. This blogpost will be centered around a PoC that I have constructed.

Our attack can be broken down into roughly 8 steps.

  1. Forge fake chunk and doubly linked list for unsorted bin for valid chunk consolidation.
  2. Use overflow primitive to override prev_in_use bit of chunk, allowing it to consolidate with fake chunk, upgrading our primitive to overlapping chunks.
  3. Remainder large overlapped chunk within unsorted bin, then use our overlapping chunks primitive to override size of first remaindered chunk. Freeing the new (larger) chunk, we can now repeat the remaindering, upgrading our primitive to a use-after-free.
  4. Forge fake chunk metadata at &tcache_perthread_struct + 0x20080
  5. Forge fake chunk header in tcache_perthread_struct
  6. Forge doubly linked list pointers for tcache_perthread_struct
  7. Using our UAF primitive in step 3, partial override pointers in unsorted bin doubly linked list to point to our fake chunk within tcache_perthread_struct
  8. Modify entries within tcache_perthread_struct, giving us leakless control over next allocations!

Throughout this blog post, chunk sizes will be taken to mean chunk sizes including metadata.

Obtaining overlapping chunks

Heap grooming

We want to construct a heap that looks like this first. We create a buffer to set the prev chunk to start at an address with LSB of 0000. We are making the assumption heap starts with LSB 0000 (this is required as our second LSB must be 00 due to null termination), which is only valid 1/16 of the time, as only last 3 nibbles are unaffected by ASLR. So this is not a 100% reliable exploit, requires 4-bit bruteforce.

Chunks a, b, and prev will be used in the first section of our attack. prev will be made to overlap with a fake chunk, giving us metadata control of fake chunk later on. We select sizes of a, b, and prev such that a < prev < b, but still ensuring they are all in the same largebin. This is to have a reliable control of the order the chunks will be placed in the (sorted) doubly linked largebin list later. The guard chunks are present to prevent chunk consolidation (either with each other or with the wilderness).

The ptr chunks are used to fill in the 0x90 tcache later, so we can attack the unsorted bin with the middle chunk later on.

Obtaining code execution

But now you may be wondering, so what if I have arbitrary write, if I still don’t know where to write to? Well, the nice part about this house is that it leaves behind some pointers that point to main_arena, which happens to fall very close to the _IO_2_1_stderr_ and _IO_2_1_stdout_ structs! Unfortunately, our previous PoC only leaves some pointers at the 0x20 and 0x30 tcaches, which is nowhere near enough space to perform a FSOP attack, or even reach the FILE structs. How do we resolve this problem?

The answer is simple, instead of allocating a random large size for hacker, we can make it sized just right such that the fd and bk pointers of the remaindered fake chunk left in the unsorted bin are written to the head pointers of the 0x3e0 and 0x3f0 tcache bins!

We can do so with the size 0x1e0 like this,

However, the fd and bk pointers which now point to main_arena, as they are in the unsorted bin, point to the very start of main_arena. Our 0x3e0 write is still not enough to reach the FILE structs! So, what if we allocate a very very large chunk, which forces the fake chunk to move to the large bin?

As we know, the large bin’s head pointers are stored near the end of main_arena. So by allocating a massive chunk, the fd and bk pointers point to the very end of main_arena. This means our 0x3e0 tcache allocation is now close enough to the FILE structs to be able to attack them directly!

Doing so, the hacked chunk would be placed within attacking range of stdout and stderr! (0x230 bytes from stderr and 0x310 bytes from stdout in glibc 2.35) In fact, we have a very comfortable 416 byte write to stderr, which is more than enough for any FSOP attack! In order to leak libc pointers required for the FSOP attacks, we can simply perform partial override of _IO_write_base in stdout, allowing for libc pointers and hence libc base to be leaked.

Conclusion

I hope this post has helped you understand the House of Water attack and Poison Null Byte, along with how they can be combined together to craft beautiful attacks. The nice thing about House of Water is that so long as you have some way to obtain control of chunk metadata, be it through UAF, double free, or formation of overlapping chunks through another house, you can make use of it quite effectively. Personally, I really really enjoy heap challenges that involve upgrading your incredibly weak primitives to increasingly stronger ones, and I hope that in the age of AI and CTFs getting autosolved, we can still appreciate the beauty and profundities of the techniques that challenge authors create their challenges based off.
